\begin{abstract}
% TODO (~150-200 palavras). Cobrir, nesta ordem:
%  - motivação: detectores de deepfake de voz são caixas-pretas SSL treinadas
%    majoritariamente em inglês; pouco se sabe sobre QUAIS pistas acústicas eles
%    usam em pt-BR, e se essas pistas são enviesadas pelo idioma.
%  - o que foi feito: explicabilidade acústica POPULACIONAL de um detector
%    wav2vec 2.0 / XLS-R no BRSpeech-DF, contrastando o modelo zero-shot (D_zs)
%    com uma adaptação leve (D_ad: XLS-R congelado + cabeça de regressão logística).
%  - como: duas espinhas complementares -- (1) associação de Spearman intra-classe
%    sobre 26 features MFCC (associativa) e (2) oclusão causal espectral por bandas
%    mel (intervencionista), com uma camada confirmatória (Welch/Levene + FDR).
%  - achado principal: preencher depois de rodar a pipeline no ambiente final
%    (como as pistas se deslocam de D_zs para D_ad).
State-of-the-art audio deepfake detectors are self-supervised black boxes trained
overwhelmingly on English anti-spoofing data, leaving open which acoustic cues they
actually exploit when applied to other languages such as Brazilian Portuguese. We
present a population-level acoustic explainability study of a wav2vec~2.0 / XLS-R
detector on the BRSpeech-DF corpus, contrasting the zero-shot model
($D_\mathrm{zs}$) with a lightly adapted variant ($D_\mathrm{ad}$: frozen XLS-R
plus a logistic read-out). We characterise the detectors along two complementary
axes over the same analysis set: an \emph{associative} axis (intra-class Spearman
correlation between 16 log-mel band-energy descriptors and the spoof score) and a
\emph{causal} axis (spectral occlusion on the \emph{same} mel-band grid, with
per-band bootstrap confidence intervals), plus a confirmatory layer (Welch and
Levene tests under Benjamini--Hochberg control). Because both axes share one
frequency grid, we can directly quantify their agreement. Light adaptation lowers
the EER from 25.8\% to 20.9\% and reshapes the cues: the causal spectral footprint
changes from a diffuse, broadband pattern (occluding almost any band lowers the
spoof score) to a localised low-frequency reliance with an emergent mid-band cue
that favours bonafide, while the associative profile weakens and moves toward
mid-band energy. For the zero-shot detector the causally influential bands and the
most associated ones broadly coincide (band-wise Spearman $\rho=0.69$), a
correspondence that adaptation largely removes ($\rho=0.10$). Error-linked acoustic
differences are statistically significant after FDR correction for both detectors.
All results are reproducible from fixed seeds and released code.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% Spec §1 (motivação e pergunta de pesquisa). Traduzir/expandir, não copiar.
% Pontos a cobrir:
%  - deepfakes de voz e a escassez de análise INTERPRETÁVEL em pt-BR;
%  - detectores SSL (XLS-R) são caixas-pretas treinadas majoritariamente em
%    inglês/ASVspoof -> risco de pistas enviesadas pela fonética do idioma;
%  - a pergunta de pesquisa (spec §1):
%      "Quais características acústicas o detector usa para decidir, e como essas
%       pistas mudam antes vs. depois de uma adaptação leve ao português?"
%  - por que contrastar zero-shot (D_zs) vs. adaptado leve (D_ad);
%  - contribuições (bullet list de 3-4 itens), p.ex.:
%      (i) protocolo de explicabilidade acústica populacional para detectores
%          caixa-preta de deepfake em pt-BR;
%      (ii) evidência complementar associativa (Spearman/MFCC) + causal (oclusão
%           espectral) sobre o MESMO conjunto de análise;
%      (iii) medição de como as pistas se deslocam com adaptação leve;
%      (iv) tabelas/figuras reprodutíveis (código e seeds fixas).
Synthetic and manipulated speech has become cheap to produce and hard to detect by
ear, motivating automatic anti-spoofing detectors. The strongest of these are built
on self-supervised learning (SSL) front-ends such as wav2vec~2.0 / XLS-R, which
achieve remarkable accuracy but behave as black boxes. Crucially, they are trained
overwhelmingly on English-centric benchmarks (e.g.\ ASVspoof), so when deployed on
other languages they may rely on cues that are entangled with the phonetics of the
training language rather than with genuine artefacts of synthesis. For Brazilian
Portuguese (pt-BR) in particular, little is known about \emph{which acoustic
properties} drive a detector's decision, or whether those properties are
language-biased.

We therefore ask: \emph{which acoustic cues does an SSL deepfake detector rely on
to decide, and how do those cues change before versus after a light adaptation to
Brazilian Portuguese?} To answer this without retraining the representation, we
compare two detectors that share the same frozen XLS-R front-end --- a zero-shot
detector ($D_\mathrm{zs}$) using the checkpoint's original head, and a lightly
adapted detector ($D_\mathrm{ad}$) that retrains only a logistic read-out on pt-BR
embeddings. This design isolates the effect of adaptation on the \emph{use} of
existing representations, providing a controlled ``before/after'' contrast for
acoustic explanation.

Our contributions are: (i)~a reproducible protocol for \emph{population-level}
acoustic explainability of black-box deepfake detectors in pt-BR; (ii)~two
complementary lines of evidence --- \emph{associative} (intra-class Spearman
correlation over log-mel band-energy descriptors) and \emph{causal} (spectral
occlusion with bootstrap uncertainty) --- computed on the \emph{same} analysis set
and, crucially, on a \emph{shared} mel-frequency grid so their agreement is
directly measurable; (iii)~a measurement of how these cues shift under light
adaptation; and (iv)~fully reproducible tables and figures with fixed seeds and
released code.

% --- Research questions and hypotheses ------------------------------------
% Registro de linguagem (spec §11, decisão do autor): H1 e H2 são reportadas de
% forma DESCRITIVA (sem alegação de significância no contraste entre detectores);
% apenas H3 é INFERENCIAL (teste formal + FDR). H2 é a única que admite linguagem
% causal (intervenção no áudio); H1 permanece associativa (Spearman intra-classe).
% NOTA: a Espinha 1 usa correlação de Spearman (não mais surrogate+SHAP), evitando
% a dependência de fidelidade de um modelo substituto.
We frame the study around one research question: \emph{which acoustic cues does
the detector rely on, and how do these cues shift after a light adaptation to
Brazilian Portuguese?} We evaluate two detectors sharing a frozen XLS-R
front-end: a zero-shot detector ($D_{\mathrm{zs}}$) and a lightly adapted one
($D_{\mathrm{ad}}$).

One condition must hold for the comparison to be meaningful: adaptation must
improve detection. We require $\mathrm{EER}(D_{\mathrm{ad}}) <
\mathrm{EER}(D_{\mathrm{zs}})$ before any cue analysis; otherwise the
``before/after'' contrast is reported as \emph{insufficient adaptation}. Because
our associative analysis is a direct, model-agnostic rank correlation (rather than
attributions from a fitted surrogate), it carries no separate surrogate-fidelity
precondition.

We investigate three hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{H1 (associative cue shift).} The acoustic-association profile
        over log-mel band-energy features differs between $D_{\mathrm{zs}}$ and
        $D_{\mathrm{ad}}$. We measure association as the \emph{intra-class Spearman
        rank correlation} between each band-energy descriptor and
        $P(\mathrm{spoof})$ (FDR-controlled per detector), and compare the profiles
        \emph{descriptively} across detectors; we make no significance claim on the
        between-detector contrast.
  \item \textbf{H2 (causal spectral shift).} The frequency bands whose occlusion
        reduces $P(\mathrm{spoof})$ differ between $D_{\mathrm{zs}}$ and
        $D_{\mathrm{ad}}$. Because occlusion is an intervention on the input, we
        report the resulting causal profiles \emph{with quantified uncertainty}
        (95\% bootstrap confidence intervals on the per-band mean drop): a band
        whose interval excludes zero exerts a reliable causal effect on the
        score. We stop short of a formal significance test across detectors.
  \item \textbf{H3 (error-linked acoustic differences).} For the band-energy
        features most salient by intra-class Spearman $|\rho|$, distributions
        differ across decision quadrants:
        in mean between true-negative and false-positive clips (Welch's
        $t$-test) and in variance between true-positive and false-negative clips
        (Levene's test). H3 is tested \emph{inferentially}, controlling the
        false discovery rate (Benjamini--Hochberg, $q<0.05$).
\end{itemize}

Because H1 and H2 are expressed on the \emph{same} mel-band grid, we additionally
quantify their \emph{agreement} for each detector as the band-wise Spearman
correlation between the causal occlusion profile and the associative $|\rho|$
profile: a high value means the bands the detector causally relies on are also the
ones most associated with its score.

\section{Related Work}
% Os papers-base (ver references.bib) reposicionados para o eixo de
% EXPLICABILIDADE acústica (não mais robustez/GATR do notebook 1). Deixar claro
% que este é um estudo empírico de interpretabilidade, complementar à revisão
% sistemática PRISMA (ENIAC 2026) já feita pelo grupo -- sem sobreposição.
\subsection{Self-Supervised Front-Ends for Anti-Spoofing}
% Front-ends SSL (wav2vec 2.0 / XLS-R) como base de detectores SOTA.
\citet{tak2022automatic} established the now-dominant recipe for spoofing and
deepfake detection: a wav2vec~2.0 self-supervised front-end, fine-tuned with
aggressive data augmentation, feeding a lightweight back-end classifier. This
family of models --- and its multilingual XLS-R variants --- underpins most
state-of-the-art anti-spoofing systems and is the class of detector we study. Their
strength, however, comes with opacity: the learned representation is a high-%
dimensional black box, and the training data are predominantly English
(ASVspoof-style). Our work takes such a detector as given and asks what acoustic
information its decisions actually track when transferred to Brazilian Portuguese.

\subsection{Explainability in Audio Deepfake Detection}
% Métodos de explicabilidade aplicados a detecção de deepfake; lacuna: análise
% ACÚSTICA populacional e cross-lingual.
A growing line of work opens up these detectors. \citet{grinberg2025does} probe
what a detector attends to in the \emph{time} domain, showing that decisions can
hinge on localised temporal regions rather than on globally distributed evidence.
\citet{fullwood2025seeing} interpret how detector behaviour \emph{changes} under
data augmentation, linking training-time interventions to shifts in what the model
relies on. Our study is complementary along three axes. First, it is
\emph{spectral} rather than temporal: we localise cues in frequency via causal
occlusion. Second, it is \emph{population-level}: instead of explaining individual
clips, we characterise cue usage across the whole analysis set with statistical
uncertainty. Third, it targets a \emph{cross-lingual} setting (pt-BR) and measures
how cues move under adaptation. This empirical, interpretability-focused study is
also complementary to, and non-overlapping with, our group's prior PRISMA
systematic review.

\subsection{Cross-Lingual Generalization and Real-World Deployment}
% Generalização entre idiomas/domínios; motiva olhar pistas específicas de pt-BR.
\citet{channing2024toward} argue that robust real-world deepfake detection requires
closing an ``explainability gap'': detectors that generalise across domains and
languages must be understood, not just benchmarked. Cross-lingual transfer is
precisely where language-biased cues become a liability, since a detector tuned to
English phonetics may latch onto spurious correlates when scored on another
language. This motivates our focus on pt-BR and on \emph{how} the acoustic cues of
a fixed representation are re-weighted when the read-out is adapted to the target
language.

\section{Methodology}
% Spec §§3-8. Descrever como método científico, não como "célula X faz Y".

\subsection{Detectors Under Study}
% Spec §3. Mesmo front-end XLS-R CONGELADO nos dois; nada no XLS-R é retreinado.
%  - D_zs (zero-shot): XLS-R + cabeça original do checkpoint
%    (nii-yamagishilab/mms-300m-anti-deepfake), aplicado direto ao pt-BR.
%  - D_ad (adaptado leve): XLS-R congelado + cabeça de REGRESSÃO LOGÍSTICA
%    treinada sobre embeddings (pooling temporal médio -> vetor de 1024) em pt-BR.
%    1 áudio -> 1 vetor -> 1 score P(spoof) calibrado (sem série temporal).
% Pré-condição (spec §3): o contraste "antes/depois" só vale se D_ad melhorar o
% EER frente a D_zs; isso é verificado ANTES de qualquer análise (ver §Results).
Both detectors share the same frozen wav2vec~2.0 / XLS-R front-end
(\texttt{nii-yamagishilab/mms-300m-anti-deepfake}); nothing inside the SSL encoder
is retrained, so any behavioural difference is attributable to the read-out alone.
$D_\mathrm{zs}$ (zero-shot) applies the checkpoint's original anti-spoofing head
directly to pt-BR. $D_\mathrm{ad}$ (lightly adapted) discards that head and instead
mean-pools the frozen encoder outputs into a single 1024-dimensional embedding per
clip, on top of which we train a logistic-regression classifier that emits a
calibrated $P(\mathrm{spoof})$. Both detectors thus map one clip to one score,
enabling an identical downstream analysis. The ``before/after'' contrast is only
licensed if adaptation actually helps: we verify
$\mathrm{EER}(D_\mathrm{ad})<\mathrm{EER}(D_\mathrm{zs})$ before conducting any cue
analysis (Section~\ref{sec:results-perf}).

\subsection{Pre-processing}
% Spec §5 (fidelidade). mono -> resample 16 kHz -> janela fixa de 64.600
% amostras (~4.04 s) -> normalização (Tak et al., 2022). Crítico: os MFCCs são
% extraídos do sinal EXATAMENTE pré-processado que entra no detector, não do
% áudio bruto (o layer_norm neutraliza energia -> afeta o MFCC 0).
Each clip is converted to mono, resampled to 16\,kHz, cropped or padded to a fixed
window of 64{,}600 samples ($\approx$4.04\,s), and normalised following
\citet{tak2022automatic}. A key methodological point is that we extract acoustic
features from \emph{exactly} the pre-processed signal that enters the detector, not
from the raw waveform: the input normalisation neutralises absolute level, so
explaining the model on raw audio would misattribute cues (in particular for the
log-energy coefficient). Analysing the signal the model actually ``hears'' keeps the
explanation faithful.

\subsection{Acoustic Features (log-mel band energy)}
% Spec §5. Energia log-mel em 8 bandas (a MESMA grade da oclusão), janelas de
% 25 ms; colapso temporal por média + desvio -> 16 features por áudio.
% Escolha deliberada: viver no mesmo eixo de frequência da intervenção causal.
We summarise each clip with the \emph{log-mel band energy} on the same eight
mel-spaced bands used by the causal occlusion (Section~\ref{subsec:occlusion}),
spanning 20--7900\,Hz. On 25\,ms windows with a 10\,ms hop we integrate the STFT
power within each band, take the logarithm, and collapse the temporal axis into two
statistics per band --- the mean ($\mu$) and the standard deviation ($\sigma$) ---
yielding $8\times2=16$ features. The means capture how much energy sits in each part
of the spectrum, while the standard deviations capture temporal \emph{variability}
within the clip, which turns out to be discriminative for the error analysis. This
choice is deliberate: unlike cepstral coefficients, band energies are localised in
frequency, so the associative analysis operates on the \emph{same} axis as the
causal occlusion analysis and the two can be compared band by band.

\subsection{Associative Analysis (Spearman)}
\label{subsec:assoc}
% Espinha 1 é ASSOCIATIVA e model-agnostic (substitui o antigo surrogate+SHAP,
% cuja fidelidade era baixa). Para cada detector (D_zs, D_ad):
%  1) medir a correlação de Spearman entre cada MFCC (26 features) e o score
%     contínuo P(spoof), CALCULADA DENTRO DE CADA CLASSE (bonafide/spoof) para
%     evitar correlação espúria induzida pela separação de classes;
%  2) controlar FDR (Benjamini-Hochberg) sobre os p-valores por detector;
%  3) comparar os perfis de |rho| por MFCC entre D_zs e D_ad (descritivo).
% Vantagem: sem modelo substituto, não há pré-condição de fidelidade; a medida é
% transparente (correlação de posto) ao custo de ser univariada (sem interações).
The associative analysis measures, for each detector, how each band-energy
descriptor is associated with the continuous score $P(\mathrm{spoof})$. We use Spearman's rank
correlation $\rho$, computed \emph{within each class} (bonafide and spoof
separately). The intra-class restriction is essential: because the score separates
the two classes, pooling them would manufacture a large spurious correlation for
almost any feature that merely differs between classes. Within a class, $\rho$
instead reflects genuine monotonic covariation between the band energy and the
detector's confidence. We control the false discovery rate across features per
detector (Benjamini--Hochberg) and compare the resulting $|\rho|$ profiles between
$D_\mathrm{zs}$ and $D_\mathrm{ad}$ \emph{descriptively}. This estimator is
model-agnostic and transparent --- it needs no fitted surrogate and therefore has no
fidelity precondition --- at the cost of being univariate (it does not model feature
interactions).

\subsection{Causal Analysis (Spectral Occlusion)}
\label{subsec:occlusion}
% Spec §7. Banco de bandas espaçadas em mel aplicadas como filtro band-stop
% sobre a forma de onda (evita inversão mel->áudio, lossy). Oclui-se uma banda
% por vez, passa-se pelo PRÓPRIO detector e mede-se a queda de P(spoof)
% (baseline = áudio íntegro). Subconjunto estratificado por quadrante.
% Evidência intervencionista sobre a caixa-preta (validade convergente com a
% Espinha 1, que é associativa) -- eixos diferentes: bandas de frequência vs.
% coeficientes MFCC.
% NOTA de implementação (do notebook): o áudio é reamostrado para 16 kHz ANTES
% do band-stop, garantindo band edges < Nyquist (8 kHz).
The causal analysis intervenes directly on the input. We partition the spectrum into
eight mel-spaced bands spanning 20--7900\,Hz and, one band at a time, remove it with
a band-stop filter applied to the waveform (operating on the waveform avoids the
lossy mel$\rightarrow$audio inversion). The filtered clip is passed through the
\emph{same} detector, and we record the drop in $P(\mathrm{spoof})$ relative to the
intact-audio baseline; a positive drop means the band was \emph{sustaining} the
spoof score. We apply this to a quadrant-stratified subset (600 clips per band) and
quantify uncertainty with a per-band bootstrap (95\% CI on the mean drop): a band
whose interval excludes zero exerts a reliable causal effect. Because occlusion is
an intervention on the audio, the occlusion analysis yields interventional evidence
that converges with --- but is methodologically independent of --- the associative
analysis. Crucially, both analyses are defined on the \emph{same} eight bands, so
this agreement can be measured band by band rather than merely asserted (see
Section~\ref{subsec:convergence}). For implementation safety, audio is resampled to
16\,kHz before filtering, keeping all band edges below the 8\,kHz Nyquist limit.

\subsection{Convergence of the Two Analyses}
\label{subsec:convergence}
% Concordância causal x associativa na MESMA grade: Spearman entre |queda de
% oclusão| por banda e max|rho| associativo por banda (por detector).
Because the associative and causal analyses share one mel-band grid, we summarise
their agreement per detector with a single band-wise Spearman correlation between the
causal profile (the magnitude of the mean occlusion drop per band) and the
associative profile (the per-band maximum $|\rho|$ over statistics and classes). With
only eight bands this correlation is a descriptive summary rather than a powered
test; a high value indicates that the bands the detector causally relies on are also
those most associated with its score.

\subsection{Confirmatory Statistics}
% Spec §8. Em vez de 26 testes-T soltos (pescaria), os testes clássicos rodam
% APENAS nos poucos MFCCs de maior |rho| (destacados pela Espinha 1), com correção
% FDR (Benjamini-Hochberg):
%  - Welch (diferença de média) e Levene/Brown-Forsythe (diferença de variância)
%    entre quadrantes relevantes (VN vs. FP; VP vs. FN);
%  - Spearman entre MFCC e P(spoof) INTRA-classe (evita correlação espúria).
To avoid a fishing expedition over all 16 loosely motivated $t$-tests, the
confirmatory layer restricts formal testing to the few band-energy features already
flagged as most salient by the associative analysis (largest intra-class $|\rho|$).
On those features
we run two contrasts tied
to the detector's error structure: a Welch $t$-test for a \emph{mean} difference
between correctly and incorrectly rejected clips (TN vs.\ FP), and a Levene test for
a \emph{variance} difference between caught and missed spoofs (TP vs.\ FN). All
$p$-values are corrected for multiple comparisons with the Benjamini--Hochberg
procedure, and we report significance as $q<0.05$. This is the only part of the
study framed inferentially; the associative and causal analyses remain descriptive.

\paragraph{Dataset and sampling rationale.}
BRSpeech-DF is a large-scale Brazilian-Portuguese anti-spoofing corpus
(459{,}137 clips: 76{,}644 bonafide and 382{,}493 spoofed, the latter carrying a
generator-method attribute), split into \texttt{train}/\texttt{validation}/%
\texttt{test}. Its \texttt{test} partition alone holds 1{,}320 bonafide and
6{,}587 spoofed clips. Because our objective is population-level acoustic
\emph{explainability} rather than leaderboard-style benchmarking, we deliberately
analyse a compact, class-controlled subset instead of the full corpus. We take the
\emph{entire} bonafide test split (1{,}320 clips --- the scarce class) matched
with 1{,}500 spoofed test clips, and fit the $D_\mathrm{ad}$ logistic head on an
independent, class-balanced training subset (1{,}500 per class); test clips are
never seen during adaptation, precluding leakage. This scale is a design choice,
not a data limitation. First, every inferential claim (H3: Welch and Levene under
Benjamini--Hochberg control) and every interval estimate (H2: per-band bootstrap
CIs) is already well powered at this $N$: at the EER operating point the rare error
quadrants (FP, FN) still retain a few hundred clips each, enough to resolve
small-to-moderate effects ($d\!\approx\!0.25$--$0.3$ at 80\% power), so additional
clips would add negligible statistical resolution. Second, exhausting the scarce
bonafide class while keeping a comparable spoof sample keeps the four decision
quadrants (TP/TN/FP/FN) and the EER threshold meaningful, which the corpus's native
$\approx$1:5 bonafide/spoof imbalance would otherwise distort. Third, the per-clip
analysis (band-stop spectral occlusion with a per-band bootstrap) scales with $N$,
so a focused subset preserves full reproducibility at tractable cost. Scaling the analysis to the full corpus and
stratifying it by spoof generator is left to future work.

\section{Explainability Analysis}

\subsection{Acoustic Association via Spearman}
% Testa H1 (deslocamento associativo). Registro DESCRITIVO no contraste entre
% detectores; cada rho é FDR-controlado. Linguagem ASSOCIATIVA ("está associado
% a"), NUNCA causal. Medida = Spearman intra-classe (bonafide/spoof separados).
% Figura association_profile_zs_vs_ad: dois paineis (energia de banda mu e desvio
% sigma), forca = max|rho| sobre classes; eixo x = bandas ordenadas por frequencia.
% O scatter de Spearman (fig:spearman) detalha a associacao por classe da banda
% dominante.
\begin{figure}[tbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{association_profile_zs_vs_ad}
  \caption{Acoustic-association profile of the log-mel band-energy features,
  measured as the intra-class Spearman $|\rho|$ between each band descriptor and
  $P(\mathrm{spoof})$ (maximum over the bonafide/spoof classes), split into mean
  ($\mu$, top) and standard-deviation ($\sigma$, bottom) descriptors and ordered by
  frequency band along the $x$-axis. We read the between-detector shift
  \emph{descriptively} (H1).}
  \label{fig:association}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:association} compares the per-band association profiles of
$D_\mathrm{zs}$ and $D_\mathrm{ad}$. For the zero-shot detector, the score is most
strongly associated with \emph{low-band energy means}: the 20--282\,Hz band
(intra-class $\rho=+0.45$ in spoof, $+0.39$ in bonafide) and the 282--639\,Hz band
($\rho=+0.41$ in both classes), with secondary mid-band contributions at
1.79--2.69 and 2.69--3.93\,kHz ($\rho\approx+0.38$ in spoof). All are positive: more
band energy co-varies with a higher spoof score. Under adaptation the profile both
\emph{weakens} and \emph{moves upward in frequency}: $D_\mathrm{ad}$'s strongest
associations are mid-band energy means that turn \emph{negative} in bonafide clips
(1.79--2.69\,kHz $\rho=-0.34$; 2.69--3.93\,kHz $\rho=-0.33$; 1.13--1.79\,kHz
$\rho=-0.29$), together with mid-band energy \emph{variability} in spoof clips
(2.69--3.93\,kHz\,$\sigma$ $\rho=+0.25$). Descriptively, then, $D_\mathrm{zs}$
is associated mainly with low-band energy, whereas $D_\mathrm{ad}$'s (weaker)
univariate associations sit in the mid band. All associations cited above are FDR-significant
per detector ($q\ll0.01$); we make no significance claim on the between-detector
contrast itself.

\subsection{Spectral Occlusion Profiles}
% Testa H2 (deslocamento causal). Incerteza QUANTIFICADA (IC 95% bootstrap por
% banda): "banda cujo IC exclui zero tem efeito causal confiável"; sem teste de
% significância formal entre detectores. Linguagem CAUSAL para o efeito da
% intervenção ("ocluir a banda X derruba P(spoof)").
% Figura occlusion_bands_zs_vs_ad: perfil DIVERGENTE por detector (paineis
% empilhados D_zs/D_ad) -- area vermelha = banda sustenta spoof (ocluir derruba
% P(spoof)); azul = sustenta bonafide (ocluir sobe P(spoof)); faixa cinza = IC 95%.
% Tabela occlusion_table: detector, band_hz_low, band_hz_high, mean_p_spoof_drop,
% ci_low, ci_high, n.
\begin{figure}[tbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{occlusion_bands_zs_vs_ad}
  \caption{Divergent spectral occlusion profiles ($D_\mathrm{zs}$ top,
  $D_\mathrm{ad}$ bottom). Red area: occluding the band lowers $P(\mathrm{spoof})$,
  so the band \emph{contributes to the spoof decision}; blue: occlusion raises it,
  so the band \emph{contributes to a bonafide decision}; grey ribbon: 95\% bootstrap
  CI of the per-band mean drop. Bands whose CI excludes zero have a reliable causal
  effect (H2).}
  \label{fig:occlusion_bands}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[tbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{occlusion_overlay_zs_vs_ad}
  \caption{Direct overlay of the two mean occlusion profiles with 95\% bootstrap
  CI ribbons, easing the band-by-band $D_\mathrm{zs}$ vs.\ $D_\mathrm{ad}$
  comparison.}
  \label{fig:occlusion_overlay}
\end{figure}

Figures~\ref{fig:occlusion_bands} and~\ref{fig:occlusion_overlay} report the
causal spectral profiles (each band occluded on 600 clips; 95\% bootstrap CI).
For $D_\mathrm{zs}$ the effect is broadband and one-signed: occluding almost any
band lowers $P(\mathrm{spoof})$ --- seven of eight bands have a CI excluding zero
(all but 639--1125\,Hz) --- so energy across the whole spectrum contributes to the
spoof decision. The effect is largest at low frequencies (20--639\,Hz, mean drop
$\approx0.04$) and at 1788--2693\,Hz ($0.037$). Adaptation turns this diffuse pattern
into a band-selective one and reverses one mid band: for $D_\mathrm{ad}$ the positive
contribution concentrates at 20--282\,Hz ($0.060$) and 639--1125\,Hz ($0.052$), the
282--639\,Hz band becomes null (CI includes zero), and 1125--1788\,Hz \emph{changes
sign} (mean drop $=-0.016$, CI $[-0.025,-0.007]$): occluding it \emph{raises}
$P(\mathrm{spoof})$, so that band instead contributes to a bonafide decision. In
causal terms, adaptation concentrates the detector's spectral reliance on low
frequencies and introduces a mid-band cue that favours bonafide, absent in the
zero-shot model.

\subsection{Convergence of the Two Analyses}
% Figura spine_convergence_zs_vs_ad: barras = |queda causal| por banda; linha =
% |rho| associativo por banda (eixo direito); titulo traz o rho de concordancia.
\begin{figure}[tbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{spine_convergence_zs_vs_ad}
  \caption{Agreement between the associative and causal analyses on the shared
  mel-band grid ($D_\mathrm{zs}$ top, $D_\mathrm{ad}$ bottom). Bars: magnitude of the
  causal occlusion drop per band; line (right axis): associative $|\rho|$ per band.
  Each title reports the band-wise Spearman correlation between the two profiles.}
  \label{fig:convergence}
\end{figure}

Because both analyses live on the same eight bands, we can ask whether the bands the
detector \emph{causally} relies on are the same ones its score is \emph{associated}
with (Figure~\ref{fig:convergence}). For the zero-shot detector the two profiles are
broadly consistent: the band-wise Spearman correlation is $\rho=0.69$, with both the
causal drops and the associations largest in the low bands. With only eight bands
this is suggestive rather than conclusive ($p=0.06$, not significant at the $0.05$
level), so we read it as descriptive support rather than proof. For the adapted
detector the correspondence essentially vanishes ($\rho=0.10$, $p=0.82$): its causal
footprint (low bands plus a mid-band cue that favours bonafide) is no longer tracked
by the univariate association ranking. We do not speculate on the source of this
divergence; we simply note that, for $D_\mathrm{ad}$, band-level claims should rest
on the causal analysis rather than the associative one.

  band-energy features across decision

Figure~\ref{fig:confirmatory_box} shows the quadrant-wise distributions with
FDR-corrected significance. Testing the ten most $|\rho|$-salient band-energy
features across decision quadrants, 9 of 20 tests are significant after
Benjamini--Hochberg correction ($q<0.05$) for $D_\mathrm{zs}$ and 13 of 20 for

(TN vs.\ FP, Welch) is largest for the low-band energy means in $D_\mathrm{zs}$

--- and their variability (20--282\,Hz\,$\sigma$: $t=+13.3$; 282--639\,Hz\,$\sigma$:
$t=+12.1$). In $D_\mathrm{ad}$ the same low-band variability (20--282\,Hz\,$\sigma$:
$t=+6.8$) is joined by \emph{mid-band} energy means (1.79--2.69\,kHz: $t=+6.7$;
1.13--1.79\,kHz: $t=+6.7$; 2.69--3.93\,kHz: $t=+6.1$), echoing the mid-band shift
seen in the association and occlusion profiles. The variance contrast between caught
and missed spoofs (TP vs.\ FN, Levene) is dominated by the low-band energy
variability 20--282\,Hz\,$\sigma$ (Levene $W=33.6$, $q=2{\times}10^{-8}$ for
$D_\mathrm{zs}$; $W=72.8$, $q=7{\times}10^{-16}$ for $D_\mathrm{ad}$): spoof clips
the detector misses carry markedly different low-band variability from those it
catches. These tests significantly link specific frequency bands to the detectors'
error structure, with low-band variability recurring for both detectors.

As a complementary associative view, Figure~\ref{fig:spearman} relates the most
correlated band-energy feature to the detector score within each class
(\texttt{spearman\_table}). The association is monotonic and class-dependent. For
$D_\mathrm{zs}$ the low-band energy mean (20--282\,Hz) rises with

score.
For $D_\mathrm{ad}$ the dominant cue is the mid-band energy mean (1.79--2.69\,kHz):
it \emph{lowers} the score among bonafide clips ($\rho=-0.34$, $q<10^{-34}$) yet is
essentially flat among spoof clips ($\rho=+0.06$), showing the cue acts chiefly by
\emph{depressing the score of genuine speech} rather than by discriminating within
the spoof class. Computing $\rho$ within each class is therefore essential: pooling
the classes would manufacture a spurious global correlation from the score's
bonafide/spoof separation alone.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
% Spec §11 (riscos, limitações, calibração de linguagem):
%  - não misturar registro associativo (Espinha 1) e causal (Espinha 2);
%  - interpretação MFCC->fonema é frouxa (usar como pista, não prova);
%  - quadrantes raros (FP/FN) com N baixo -> ressalvar poder;
%  - adaptação insuficiente: se D_ad não superar D_zs, o contraste perde força;
%  - janela fixa de ~4 s; um único checkpoint e um único dataset pt-BR.
Several caveats bound the interpretation of these results. \emph{Registers do not
mix.} The associative analysis and the causal analysis answer different questions;
we keep their language separate and treat their agreement as corroboration rather
than as a single proof. Only H3 is stated inferentially. \emph{Adaptation is shallow by
design.} $D_\mathrm{ad}$ retrains only a linear read-out on a frozen encoder, so the
observed cue shifts reflect a \emph{re-weighting} of pre-existing XLS-R
representations, not new features learned for pt-BR; a fully fine-tuned encoder could
move cues further, and our conclusions should not be extrapolated to that regime.
\emph{Occlusion has a confound.} Band-stop filtering both removes a band's content
and injects filtering artefacts; a detector might react to the distortion itself
rather than to the missing band, so an occlusion effect localises \emph{where} the
model is sensitive without proving the band's content is the sole cause.
\emph{Band energy is not phonetics.} Log-mel band energies index where spectral
power sits, not phonemes; we use them as acoustic pointers, not as phonetic proof.
\emph{The associative analysis is univariate.} Spearman correlations score one band
at a time and cannot capture cross-band interactions. Because its $D_\mathrm{ad}$
profile no longer tracks the causal one (band-wise agreement $\rho=0.10$), we base
band-level claims about $D_\mathrm{ad}$ on the occlusion analysis. \emph{Power in
rare quadrants.} The FP and FN quadrants, though numbering in the hundreds, are the
smallest, so small effects there may go undetected. Finally, the study uses a single
checkpoint, a single pt-BR corpus, and a fixed $\approx$4\,s window; generalisation
across models, languages, and durations remains to be established.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
% Síntese (spec §9 passo 9) + extensões opcionais (spec §12):
%  - análise por algoritmo gerador (coluna 'model' do config spoof -> ANOVA);
%  - correlação embedding<->MFCC (análise representacional).
We presented a population-level, dual-axis acoustic explanation of a black-box
XLS-R deepfake detector on Brazilian Portuguese, contrasting a zero-shot model with
a lightly adapted one. Associative (intra-class Spearman) and causal (spectral
occlusion) evidence, computed on a shared mel-band grid, agree that light adaptation
reshapes the detector's acoustic reliance: from a diffuse, broadband use of
low-band spectral energy toward a localised low-frequency footprint with an emergent
mid-band cue that favours bonafide, alongside a clear EER improvement. Placing both
analyses on the same frequency axis let us quantify their agreement directly: it is
moderate for the zero-shot detector (band-wise $\rho=0.69$, though only suggestive at
eight bands) and essentially absent after adaptation ($\rho=0.10$), locating where
the two views of the model agree and where they do not. Error-linked acoustic
differences are statistically significant under FDR control for both detectors. Future work includes stratifying the analysis by spoof generator (the
corpus exposes a generator-method attribute, enabling per-algorithm ANOVA), scaling
to the full corpus, correlating band-energy descriptors with the XLS-R embedding
space to bridge acoustic and representational views, and extending the protocol to
fully fine-tuned detectors and additional languages.
